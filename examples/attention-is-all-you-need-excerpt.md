# Demo Excerpt: Attention Is All You Need

This short example demonstrates the report format without reproducing the source paper. It is based on the public paper linked in `evals/evals.json` and uses only section labels and generalized observations.

## Evidence-led observation

| Location | Observable fact | Interpretation | Transferable rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Abstract | The abstract moves from a broad task limitation to a proposed architecture and then a quantitative outcome. | The contribution is framed as a replacement for a familiar bottleneck before its name is introduced. | Use a five-step abstract: context, constraint, method, distinguishing mechanism, measurable outcome. |
| Introduction | The opening establishes the dominant family of approaches before narrowing to sequential-computation cost. | The gap is specific enough to justify a structural alternative, not merely an incremental optimization. | State the standard approach fairly, then isolate one constraint that the proposed design directly removes. |
| Method | Formula blocks follow local symbol definitions and are followed by explanation of their role. | Mathematical notation is introduced only after the reader knows the question each formula answers. | Precede formulas with purpose and notation; follow them with an operational interpretation. |

## Writing blueprint excerpt

### Introduction

1. Establish the established solution family and its value.
2. Narrow to a concrete limitation affecting scalability or capability.
3. Explain why common alternatives do not fully remove that limitation.
4. Introduce the proposed architecture in one declarative contribution sentence.
5. Preview the evidence that will validate the claim.

Use present tense for accepted knowledge, present perfect or past tense for prior work where appropriate, and cautious claims until empirical evidence has been introduced.
